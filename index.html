Garment Studio Guide

Make clothes in Blender the way you would in Marvelous Designer: draw flat pattern pieces, sew their edges, place them on an avatar and let the cloth drape. Every clip and picture here was recorded in Blender with Garment Studio 1.6.3; the fixes since, 1.6.4 and 1.6.5, are for pairs of pieces (X Mirror and the Knife) and change nothing shown.

Get started

From an empty file to a draped T-shirt

Four steps, the same ones the Get Started panel shows at the top of the Garment tab while a file has no avatar or no pieces.

You need

Blender 5.2 on Windows, with an NVIDIA graphics card. The physics engine comes inside the add-on; nothing else to install.

Install

Edit › Preferences › Get Extensions, the ⌄ menu at the top right, Install from Disk, and pick garment_studio-1.6.5.zip.

Step 1

Open Garment Studio

  1. In any 3D view press N: the sidebar's Garment tab has Open Garment Studio.
  2. It makes the Garment workspace: the 3D window on the left, the pattern window on the right, each with its Tools at the left and its panels in the sidebar.
  3. Deleted it? The same button makes it again.

Step 2

An avatar to dress

  1. Get Started, step 1: pick your own character in Your Own, or Add the Test Mannequin.
  2. The Avatar panel, in both sidebars, sets how the avatar looks (Solid, See-Through, Hidden) and its height.

Step 3

Pattern pieces

  1. Step 2: Add a T-Shirt, or More Garments for the others. Or draw your own piece with Rectangle in the Tools.
  2. The pieces lie flat in the pattern window and start wrapped round the avatar in 3D.

Step 4

Simulate

  1. Step 3: Simulate, Space, or the play button in the 3D window's tool settings row.
  2. The pieces sew shut and drape. Space again pauses; Reset puts the cloth back where it started.

The windows

Two windows, one garment

The 3D window on the left holds the avatar and the cloth; the pattern window on the right holds the flat pieces at real size. Each has the same parts, where Blender keeps its own.

Tools T

At the left of each window, square icons under group names. Rest the mouse on one for its name; Shift+Space then a letter picks one.

Sidebar N

The Garment tab holds the window's panels: Picked, Collections, Simulate, Physical Fabric, Triangle Size, Simulation Settings, Sewing, Check, Avatar and more. Shift+N chooses which show; Alt+N opens it on the Garment tab. Nothing opens it for you.

Task bar

Over the pattern, top centre: the buttons for what is picked. Drag its grip to move it; right-click to hide it.

Tool settings row

The tool's options, and at the right the pick's values: a piece's width, height and seam allowance, an edge's length, a seam's two sides with Even Out, Flip and Lock.

Modes and menus

Four modes, from the drop-down at the left of the pattern window's header or the Ctrl+Tab pie: Object Mode (whole pieces), Edit Mode (points, edges, lines), Sew Mode (seams) and Trims Mode (buttons, zips, cords); Tab switches Object and Edit. View, Select and Add are always there; Object Mode adds Piece and Collection, Edit Mode Point, Edge, Line and Finish, Sew Mode Sew, Trims Mode Trims. In 3D, Cloth comes after Object.

Right-click and hints

Right-click a piece, edge, point, seam or the cloth for what you can do to it. The status bar shows the keys of the tool in hand; F1 draws every key on a keyboard.

Your own layout

Garment Studio in any workspace

It works like any Blender add-on: outside its windows Blender stays as it was, and you can lay out your own.

Clip 1

Any 3D view as a pattern window

  1. In Blender's own Layout the flat pieces don't show: their collection, Pattern Pieces, has its eye closed in the outliner.
  2. View › Pattern Window: this 3D view looks down on the pattern pieces alone, with Garment Studio's Tools in it.
  3. View › Pattern Window again: the 3D view as it was.

Tutorials

Things to try, one clip each

Each clip loops. Follow the steps beside it on the T-shirt from Get started.

Tutorial 1

Pull the cloth while it simulates

  1. With Select / Move in the 3D window's Tools, drag the cloth: it follows the mouse.
  2. Let go and it settles. Hold W and click to pin a spot; Ctrl+W takes every pin off.

Tutorial 2

Pick a piece

  1. Click a piece in the pattern window. Picked in the sidebar shows its values, the task bar its buttons, the tool settings row its width, height and seam allowance.
  2. In 3D the gizmo moves it along, turns it round the body and bends it.

Tutorial 3

Draw and reshape a piece

  1. Pick Rectangle in the Tools and drag on the pattern. To type a size: width, Tab, height, Enter.
  2. Tab for Edit Mode, then drag a corner: the 3D piece follows.

Tutorial 4

Sew two edges

  1. Pick Sew Mode (the drop-down at the left of the pattern window's header, or Ctrl+Tab): Segment Sewing is in hand. Click one edge, then the edge it joins, near the ends that should meet.
  2. An uneven seam says by how much; pick it for Even Out in the tool settings row.

Tutorial 5

Place a piece on the body

  1. The arrangement points show on the avatar (Cloth › Arrangement Points, or its button in the 3D window's header).
  2. Pick a piece, click a point: it wraps on there. While the cloth simulates, that piece starts again from there and the rest carries on.

Tutorial 6

Cut while it simulates

  1. In Edit Mode (Tab), pick the Knife in the Tools and drag across a piece: it is cut in two and the cut sewn back.
  2. The piece cut off keeps its place: here the Back's hem strip stays at the hem, and starts on the cloth where it was.

Every operation

Operations, chapter by chapter

Each clip acts one operation out in Blender. The numbers drawn on it are the steps beside it, in order.

Object Mode: whole pieces

Pattern window, Object Mode

Mirror a piece

  1. Right-click the piece, in Object Mode.
  2. Point at Mirror.
  3. Click Flip X: the piece turns over where it lies, its seams kept.

Flip Y turns it upside down. Ctrl+M is Flip X without the menu.

Pattern window, Object Mode, Select / Move

Pick and move a piece

  1. Click a piece: it is picked, outlined in orange, and its width, height and seam allowance show in the tool settings row.
  2. Drag it to move it on the board. Where it sits on the body in 3D does not change.

Shift+click picks more pieces; a drag on the empty board picks the pieces inside the box.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Copy and paste a piece

  1. Pick the piece and press Ctrl+C (or right-click, Copy).
  2. Press Ctrl+V: the copy lands beside it, with its fabric and settings, ready to move.

A pasted copy is not sewn: sew it where it belongs.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Duplicate a piece

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Click Duplicate: a copy lies beside it, picked.

Shift+D duplicates and moves the copy with the mouse until you click.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Delete a piece

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Click Delete: the piece and its seams are gone; Ctrl+Z brings them back.

X or Delete does the same to the picked pieces.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Rename a piece

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Click Rename.
  3. Type the new name and press Enter.

F2 renames the picked piece without the menu.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Give a piece another fabric

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Point at Physical Fabric.
  3. Click a fabric: the piece takes it; while the cloth simulates it changes at once.

The Physical Fabric panel in the sidebar shows the fabric's numbers; New Fabric makes one of your own.

Pattern window, Object Mode

Finer triangles on one piece

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Point at Triangle Size.
  3. Click a size: smaller triangles fold more finely and simulate more slowly.

Simulation Settings > Cloth Detail sets the size of every piece at once.

Pattern window, Object Mode; the cloth in the 3D window

Flip a piece's normals

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Click Flip Normals: its cloth in the 3D window turns its other side out for shading, at once.

Flip Wrap Direction, just under it, wraps the piece round the body the other way instead.

Pattern window, Object Mode; the cloth in the 3D window

Smooth or flat shading

  1. Right-click the piece.
  2. Click Shade Smooth: its cloth in 3D is drawn smooth; Shade Flat shows every triangle.

Pattern window, Object Mode; the cloth in the 3D window

Freeze a piece

  1. Right-click the piece while the cloth simulates.
  2. Click Freeze: it holds its shape where it is while the rest moves on (Ctrl+K).

Freeze again lets it go. A frozen piece still holds what is sewn to it.

Pattern window, Object Mode; the cloth in the 3D window

Pause one piece

  1. Right-click the piece while the cloth simulates.
  2. Click Pause: that piece stops where it is and the others carry on; Let It Simulate starts it again.

Every piece in the Collections list has its own pause button too.

Edit Mode: a piece's shape

Pattern window, Edit Mode

Move a point

  1. With the mouse over the pattern, press Tab: Edit Mode, with Edit Point in hand.
  2. Click a corner of a piece: the point is picked.
  3. Drag it: its two edges follow, and so does the piece in the 3D window.

Shift+click picks more points; G moves, R turns and S scales them, as in Blender. Tab goes back to Object Mode.

Pattern window, Edit Mode, the Edges panel

Type an edge's length

  1. In Edit Mode, click the middle of an edge: the edge is picked.
  2. In the Edges panel (sidebar, N), click Length and type the new length, then Enter.
  3. The edge takes that length. Start, Middle or End above it says which part stays put.

Angle, under it, is the edge's slope: 0 level, 90 upright. A typed angle turns the edge about the same Start, Middle or End.

Pattern window, Edit Mode

Curve an edge

  1. In Edit Mode, pick Edit Curvature in the Tools.
  2. Drag an edge's middle out or in: the edge bends into a curve, and the 3D piece follows.

Curve (%) in the Edges panel types how far the middle stands out. Edit Curve Point adds a curved point instead.

Pattern window, Edit Mode

Add a point on an edge

  1. In Edit Mode, pick Add Point in the Tools.
  2. Click an edge where the new corner should be: the point is added there.
  3. Drag the new point to shape the outline (Edit Point in hand).

Delete or X removes picked points. Right-click a point for Point Type: smooth or sharp.

Sew Mode

Pattern window, Sew Mode

Sew two edges

  1. Pick Sew Mode from the drop-down at the left of the pattern window's header (or Ctrl+Tab): Segment Sewing is in hand.
  2. Click one edge near the end that should meet the other piece.
  3. Click the second edge near its matching end: the seam is made, the same number on both edges.

An uneven seam says by how much. Free Sewing and M:N Sewing in the Tools sew parts of edges, or several edges at once.

The 3D window

3D window

Simulate, pause and reset

  1. With the mouse over the 3D window, press Space: the pieces sew shut and drape.
  2. Space again pauses; the cloth stays as it is.
  3. Reset (in the Simulate panel) puts the cloth back where it started.

While it simulates you can keep working: pick, move and edit pieces on the pattern, and the cloth follows.

Fabric, triangles and simulation

Pattern window, the Physical Fabric panel

Limit Fabric Stiffness, for every fabric

  1. Pick a piece and open the Physical Fabric panel (sidebar, N).
  2. Limit Fabric Stiffness is at its top: one setting for every fabric. On (the default), every fabric drapes smoothly with the usual solver passes; off, each keeps its own stiffness.

Off, stiff fabrics such as denim want Accurate Fabrics (Simulation Settings > Solver) for clean folds, and a collar wants Reinforce on its seam.

Limits

What it can't do yet

Known today, in 1.6.5. Each is either being worked on or waiting for a decision.

One platform
Windows with an NVIDIA graphics card, Blender 5.2. The physics engine (Newton with NVIDIA Warp) runs on the card; other systems can draw patterns but not simulate.
Layered outfits
Garments are kept apart (a jacket over a shirt settles cleanly in the test outfits), but gathered cuffs, hood corners and a trouser fly's facing can leave a few points crossing, and cloth pulled very hard can pass through itself for a moment before it heals.
A still avatar
The avatar is read when the simulation starts. Draping over a posed avatar works; cloth following an animation is not supported yet.
Final quality costs
Final with small triangles needs a lot of graphics memory and runs slower. Work in Draft and switch for the last drape.
Very thick cloth
A thick, bevelled finished mesh can show spikes at elbows and knees.
Oversized pieces
A piece scaled up far too much for its triangle size is drawn red and left out of the cloth; the Simulate panel says which and why.
One tool per workspace
Blender keeps one tool in hand for all the 3D views of a workspace. In a layout of your own, picking one of Blender's tools takes Garment Studio's out of hand in the pattern window too; click any Garment tool to take it back.
Undo while simulating
Undo takes the pattern back; the cloth carries on from where it is rather than jumping back in time.
Stiff fabrics at their own stiffness
Limit Fabric Stiffness is on by default, so every fabric drapes smoothly with the usual solver passes. Switched off (Physical Fabric panel, or Simulation Settings › Advanced), stiff fabrics such as denim or leather want Accurate Fabrics (Simulation Settings › Solver) for clean folds, and a collar wants Reinforce on its seam to stand.
Panel order in older files
Blender remembers each sidebar's panel order, so in a file from an older version a new panel can appear further down. Drag its header to move it.
Reports welcome
If the cloth ever vanishes after an undo, or something else goes wrong, open the Flight Record straight away (Simulate panel, or Help › Garment Studio: Flight Record): it lists every command since the file opened.

Is it a beta?

Yes: a beta for Blender 5.2 on Windows with NVIDIA

The whole loop works and is checked on every build. What keeps it from a 1.0 is feedback from people who didn't build it, and the gaps listed under Limits.

Ready

  • Draw, sew, place, simulate, cut, fit and finish a garment from the panels, menus and right-click, without Blender's Edit Mode.
  • Everything saves in the .blend: pieces, seams, fabrics, layers.
  • 2,065 automatic tests of the geometry and maths, and 170 scripted checks that drive Blender itself, run on every build.
  • Stress tests make dozens of edits with undo and redo after each one; a speed check holds 60 frames a second with the panels open.
  • Behaves like a Blender add-on: its own workspace, or a pattern window in any of yours.

Not yet

  • The interface has had one main tester so far: feedback from new users is what this beta is for.
  • Layered outfits still have the known gaps above.
  • One platform, and English only.
  • No feedback channel built in beyond the Flight Record.
